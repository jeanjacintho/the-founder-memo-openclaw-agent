# Patrick Salyer — enterprise software from discovery to scale

## Limits of this advice
Salyer's record is mostly enterprise/B2B SaaS, from founder-led selling and category creation through Fortune 500 buyers. His benchmarks are B2B-sales benchmarks. For consumer or bottoms-up developer businesses, state the limitation rather than forcing his numbers onto them. His published record is thin on international expansion and board management; for the first users and product before product-market fit argue from Graham, and for executives, boards, layoffs and crises from Horowitz.

## Questions that change the advice
- What pain makes the product a painkiller rather than a vitamin?
- Which users would take a reference call, and why?
- What number most constrains the company now, and where is it kept?
- Which repeatable motion has been proven beyond the founder?
- What milestone must the current runway buy?

## Stage map
- Discovery ($0–1M ARR): reach product-market fit with a painkiller and 5–10 referenceable customers.
- Blueprint ($1–10M ARR): build a founder-written, repeatable go-to-market playbook that reps can execute.
- Scale ($10M+ ARR): perfect the ramp model so the machine runs without the founder.
- An acute shock overrides ARR and puts the company in Pivot. Fundraising is a modifier alongside the operating stage.
- When signals conflict, weight ARR and founder-dependence most heavily. If concrete signals are absent, say the stage is unclear.

## Discovery
### Focus first
- Find a painful, urgent problem and 5–10 customers who will publicly vouch for the product.
- Keep the founder in every sale, implementation, and support loop long enough to learn the pattern.
### Do not focus on
- Scaling a sales team before the motion is repeatable.
### Benchmarks
- $0–1M ARR and 5–10 referenceable customers.
### Exit criteria
- A painkiller product, referenceable users, and a pattern that can be written down.

## Blueprint
### Focus first
- Write the playbook from the founder's wins, then prove a rep can run it.
### Do not focus on
- Hiring ahead of a motion the founder cannot explain.
### Benchmarks
- $1–10M ARR; reps produce roughly three times on-target earnings.
### Exit criteria
- Multiple ramped reps can attain quota using the same playbook.

## Scale
### Focus first
- Design the ramp model, org, capacity plan, and forecast so growth no longer depends on the founder.
### Do not focus on
- Founder heroics or one-off deals that cannot be repeated.
### Benchmarks
- $10M+ ARR with multiple ramped reps and reliable capacity planning.
### Exit criteria
- The system predictably adds productive capacity toward $100M ARR.

## Pivot modifier
### Signals
- A security incident, market re-rating, model-threatening technology shift, or missed milestone that blocks the next round.
### Focus first
- Name the threat, preserve runway, and test the smallest decisive change with customers.
### Do not focus on
- Defending the old story after evidence changes.
### Benchmarks
- Evidence that the new direction solves an urgent problem before scaling it.
### Exit criteria
- A newly credible customer and financing path.

## Fundraising modifier
### Signals
- Raising now or within the next one or two quarters.
### Focus first
- Tie the raise to the milestone it buys and the evidence that makes that milestone credible.
### Do not focus on
- Round labels detached from traction and the next value-inflection point.
### Benchmarks
- A competitive Series A now commonly requires roughly $3–5M ARR; treat that as context, not a universal rule.
### Exit criteria
- A sourced narrative connecting present traction, use of funds, and the next fundable milestone.
