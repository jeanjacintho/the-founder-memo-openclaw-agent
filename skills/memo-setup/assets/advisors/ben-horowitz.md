# Ben Horowitz — the CEO's job when there is no easy answer

## Limits of this advice
Horowitz writes about running a company once it has a team, a board and real stakes: hiring and firing executives, layoffs, crises, and the CEO's own psychology. He says little about finding the first users or product-market fit (Graham's ground) or about the metrics of an enterprise sales motion (Salyer's ground). Where the decision is about the product's first market, say so and argue from the advisor who covers it.

## Questions that change the advice
- Is the company facing an existential threat right now, or not?
- Which executive or role is failing, and what in the hiring process let it happen?
- Is a layoff decided, and how long until it is executed?
- What burden is the CEO carrying alone that could be shared?
- What decision is being delayed because every option is bad?

## Stage map
- Peacetime: the company has a large advantage in its market and the job is to widen it.
- Wartime: the company is fending off an imminent existential threat, and the job is to survive it.
- The Struggle is a modifier on either: the CEO doubting the company and themselves, with no easy answer.
- People decisions (hire, fire, lay off) are a modifier on either.
- When signals conflict, weight the presence of an existential threat most heavily. If there is no evidence either way, say the mode is unclear.

## Peacetime
### Focus first
- Widen the advantage: expand the market, build the culture and the processes that let many people contribute.
### Do not focus on
- Treating an ordinary setback as a war.
### Benchmarks
- None published; the signal is a clear lead in a growing market.
### Exit criteria
- A threat appears that could end the company.

## Wartime
### Focus first
- Name the threat, take the hard actions directly, and fix the product when the product is the problem.
### Do not focus on
- Protocol and consensus that slow the response; clever shortcuts instead of the hard work.
### Benchmarks
- None published; the signal is whether the threat is receding.
### Exit criteria
- The threat is beaten and the company can return to peacetime.

## The Struggle modifier
### Signals
- The CEO is questioning why they started the company, and every option looks bad.
### Focus first
- Share the burdens that can be shared; separate the decision from the fear and make it.
### Do not focus on
- Carrying everything alone, or waiting for an easy answer.
### Benchmarks
- Decisions get made instead of postponed.
### Exit criteria
- The next decision is made and owned.

## People decisions modifier
### Signals
- An executive is failing, a key hire is open, or a layoff is coming.
### Focus first
- Hire for strength rather than lack of weakness; when an executive fails, find what in the hiring process failed; once a layoff is decided, execute it fast and train the managers who deliver it.
### Do not focus on
- Delay, leaks, or blaming the person for the system's mistake.
### Benchmarks
- The time between deciding and executing is short.
### Exit criteria
- The role is filled or the change is done, and the process that caused it is fixed.
